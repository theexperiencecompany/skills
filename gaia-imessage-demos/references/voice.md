# Voice

The script is read, not heard. It has to work silently, at reading speed, for someone who does not know the product.

## The one rule

Every line carries new information or a real reaction. Nothing restates, nothing explains the product, nothing exists to set up the next line.

If a line can be removed without losing a fact, a decision, or a feeling, remove it. Warmth lines are the one exception. "Morning, Aryan." and "Good." are texture, not content. Use them sparingly and never as a substitute for a fact.

## What the founder rejected

These are the failure patterns, in the founder's words.

- "this is so ai slop"
- "the script seems boring and dead and have to think between the lines a bit. and it feels over done. its not human"
- "why would we want to have a disagreement?"
- "nah bro this is cringe lol who is this and all. the user legit signed up bro"
- "why make the reader read between the lines and vague talk like this?"
- "who tf talkes like this. drafted. 104 for retention, churn on page 3. i just fucking don't even know what this is supposed to mean."
- "still seems a bit forced like this is the problem etc and i know the problem i did this"

Each one maps to a rule.

## Readability

Name the thing. Not "the update," but "the investor update." Not "104 for retention," but "104, not 118, because two customers left." Not "it's in your inbox," but "I saved it and flagged the two clauses."

No shorthand that only the writer understands. No product terms in dialogue unless a real person would say them. No fragments that make the reader reconstruct the sentence.

The founder's positive: "okay better this way of thinking about lines being readable is great."

Test: read one line to someone who knows nothing. If they ask a question, rewrite.

## Warmth

The best surviving beat was the morning greeting. "i liked the good morning and all."

Warmth comes from ordinary care, not sentiment:

- "Morning, Aryan."
- "She'll call at 10. She always does."
- "Your mom called. She got the flowers."

Keep one personal thread across the whole piece. Resolve it at the end. Do not add a second one for decoration.

## Conflict

Ban it. "why would we want to have a disagreement?"

No arguments, no tests, no skepticism, no "prove it" beat. The tension is the deadline, the queue, the money, the thing that will go wrong if nobody acts. The assistant and the user are on the same side.

The only friction allowed is a real decision with a tradeoff, and the user makes it in one line.

## Characters

The user signed up. They know what the product is. They never ask who is texting, never marvel at the magic, never explain the premise.

They react to the work, not to the existence of the product.

Bad: "who is this"
Good: "Morning. Did the receipts go out?"

The assistant is a colleague. It does not say "I know" when the user already said it. It adds the next fact, the next action, or a correction.

## Length

Long messages for real work. Short messages for confirmations. The founder asked for longer, not clipped:

- "each convo can be slightly longer"
- "like the investor list gaia syas i can build one ten a week drafted for you to approve so like shouldnt that be longer?"
- "'book it' is too short too, doesnt see very natural."

A real request: "Book it. I'm free Thursday afternoon."
A real offer: "I can build one from funds that backed developer tools at seed. Ten a week, drafted for you to approve."

Avoid the clipped assistant register. "Drafted." "Sent." "Done." are fine once, not as a rhythm.

## Numbers

Every number has a referent and a meaning.

- Say "412 dollars", not "412".
- Say "refunded June and July, 480 dollars", not "two months back".
- Say "the 19th, 8am", not "the flight".
- If a number changed from an earlier beat, say why in the same line.

## Endings

No scoreboard, no flex, no product punchline. End on a calm human beat that proves the change.

Good: "No work today." / "None?" / "None. Your mom called. She got the flowers." / "Good."

Bad: a summary of metrics, a "we did it" line, a joke about the assistant.

## Artifacts

Artifacts are part of the conversation, not decoration. Use them where a real assistant would send one.

- A brief image at the top of the day, with the greeting chained 0.2 seconds behind it.
- A link card whose title says what the document is. "Northline meeting brief", not "Northline - one page".
- A tapback when a human would send one, from either side. Keep it to two or three in a thread.

No artifact should need a caption to explain what it is.

## The final pass

Read the whole thread at normal speed. Mark every line where you have to stop and think. Those are the failures. Rewrite them until the read is continuous.
